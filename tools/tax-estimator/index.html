How this works

  • Your profit (income minus allowable expenses) is what Income Tax and Class 4 NI are actually charged on, not your raw income.
  • The Personal Allowance (£12,570) is tax-free, and tapers away above £100,000 income, reaching zero at £125,140 — this applies across all your taxable income, not just self-employment profit, which is why "other taxable income" is factored in.
  • Class 4 National Insurance is charged on profit between £12,570 and £50,270 at 6%, and 2% above that.
  • Class 2 NI is no longer compulsory above the Small Profits Threshold (£6,725) — you get a National Insurance credit automatically. Below that threshold, paying voluntary Class 2 (about £3.45/week, roughly £180/year) can protect your State Pension record.

This is a simplified estimate using approximate current Income Tax bands and Class 4 NI rates. It doesn't account for Marriage Allowance, pension contributions, Gift Aid, student loan repayments, payments on account, or other reliefs that could change your actual bill — and rates change every tax year. Always check current figures at gov.uk (or gov.scot for Scotland) or speak to an accountant before relying on this for real tax planning.
